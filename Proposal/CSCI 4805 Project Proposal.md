# 3\. Abstract

CampusHub is a student social platform designed to help college students connect with others on campus. The primary goal is to help transfer students and students in general who may be unfamiliar with the campus or Clarksville area to meet new people, discover activities, and become more involved with other students.

CampusHub will provide a localized one place where students are able to create a profile, find and post activities, communicate with other students, and buy or sell items through a student based marketplace. By keeping the platform focused within Austin Peay State University, CampusHub's mission is to make it easier for students to build connections and become more involved with campus life.

# 5\. Feature List (Dallas)

### Features We Plan to Complete

Account Creation and Student Verification

Users will be able to create an account using their student email. A verification code will be sent to the email to make sure the user is actually a student before the account can be created. Users will then be able to sign in and out of their account. If possible, we would also like to add two-factor authentication for additional account security.

Student Profiles

After creating an account, users will be able to create and customize their profile. Profiles will include basic information such as their name, profile picture, year in school, major, and interests. This will give students a simple way to learn more about the people they are interacting with.

Activities and Posts

Campus Hub will have an activity feed where students can create posts and find things happening around campus. For example, someone could post that they are looking for people to play football, study with, go to Nashville, or attend a campus event.

When creating an activity, the user will also be able to choose who the post is intended for. For example, someone could create an activity specifically for freshmen and sophomores.

Comments and Activity Responses

Students will be able to respond to activity posts to ask questions or say that they are interested. This gives students a simple way to communicate and organize activities without having to immediately exchange personal contact information.

Student Marketplace

Campus Hub will include a marketplace where students can list items they want to sell. A listing will contain basic information such as a picture, description, and asking price.

Campus Hub will not handle payments. Buyers and sellers will communicate with each other and decide how they want to complete the sale. For example, two students could agree to meet at the library and complete the transaction in person.

Private Messaging

Users will be able to privately message other students. This will be useful for marketplace listings as well as students who meet through activities or other parts of Campus Hub. This also allows students to communicate without having to give another user their personal phone number.

The overall goal of these features is to give students one place to meet other students, find activities, and buy or sell items. This will be especially useful for transfer students who may be new to the area and do not already know many people on campus.

### Features We Will Add If There Is Time

If the main features are completed, we would like to add features such as notifications, searching for other students, more profile customization, marketplace categories, saved posts, and additional ways to filter activities.

We may also add a friend or connection system that allows students to keep track of people they meet through Campus Hub.

### Features We Would Like in the Future

There are several features that would be useful but are outside the scope of one semester. Future versions could include student groups and clubs, event calendars, more advanced recommendations, expanded moderation tools, and support for additional universities.

A future version could also explore additional marketplace features. However, processing payments directly through Campus Hub is not planned for the current project.

# 11\. Dependencies, limitations, and risks (Elijah)

## Task and Technological Dependencies:

### Accessibility Relying on Email Verification:

Accessibility to our platform relies entirely on the availability of a unique, verifiable, student email. If the email verification logic fails the features of the program will be unreachable and testing of our product will be hindered.   
To avoid this, we will have to create a tester or administrative mode that can bypass the email verification so that test accounts can be registered and features can be tested. 

### Core Features Relying on Database and Authentification:

Many core features of our program rely on having an active database and user authentication system in place. Functions such as unique student profiles, marketplace listings, private messaging, and many others will fail if any errors in our database system or authentication logic occurs. Programming the front-end of our project may be blocked if setting up our database is delayed or impeded. 

To prevent stalling our front-end work, we will prioritize configuring our database and set up an authentication provider before any other development.

## Project and Technical Limitations:

### Image Storage Quotas:

Many features we plan to implement, such as student profiles, activity posts, marketplace listings, and etc, allow the user to upload images to the database. The unrestricted storing of many high-resolution images will likely exceed the free cloud storage capacity of many services, resulting in slower page loading times and processing speeds.

This issue can generally be resolved by implementing a file-size validation system that triggers before an image can be uploaded. We could also cap the number of images allowed per post or marketplace listing, or otherwise restrict image upload. 

### User-end Browser Compatibility: 

Designing a web application for both desktop and mobile browsers can cause issues with the CSS code, address-bar resizing, malfunctioning touch-events, and degrade the over-all quality of the final product.

The best way to prevent this is to frequently check the program on both mobile and desktop without letting any discrepancies or malfunctions get out of hand. Checking the UI on both intended devices frequently will be easier than finishing the project and dealing with it afterwards. 

## General Project Risks:

### Time Constraints and Non-essential Features:

The implementation of non-essential features, like two-factor authentication, search filtering, event calendars, etc, could distract the team from more important features and result in the final product being left unpolished or dysfunctional. 

To prevent this, we will thoroughly organize our features into priority tiers and complete each tier, one at a time, without moving too far ahead. We will also establish reasonable deadlines with consideration to any unforeseen incidents for each feature; this will help us to stay on track.  
	

### Unexpected Scheduling Conflicts:

Any group project can run into issues with individual schedules, sudden illness, heavy work loads in other courses, or other unpredictable set-backs.

The solution to this is to communicate consistently and pray sincerely for a smooth ride.
